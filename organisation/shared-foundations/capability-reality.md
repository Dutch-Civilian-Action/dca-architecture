# DCA Capability Reality

## Purpose

DCA must be able to observe not only what happens in domain operations, but also what organisational capabilities actually do, maintain, change, support, depend on, and enable.

This foundation defines **Capability Reality** and its evidence boundary.

## Capability Reality

**Capability Reality** is the evidence-backed account of what an organisational capability currently does, maintains, produces, changes, supports, depends on, and enables.

It is derived from observable work and evidence. It is not inferred only from intended mandate, role descriptions, planned responsibilities, tool presence, or document existence.

## Relationship to Operational Reality

- **Operational Reality** describes what happens in DCA domain work.
- **Capability Reality** describes what an organisational capability actually does and maintains.

They may overlap at interfaces, but neither substitutes for the other.

Both may contribute to Shared Organisational Reality and may authorise later requirements when sufficiently established.

## Capability Evidence

Capability evidence may include, where relevant:

- Google Drive artifacts, revisions, routing, and maintained shared assets;
- GitHub commits, ADRs, specifications, repository changes, and implementation history;
- Slack alignment, validation, correction, dependency, publication, and use evidence;
- Airtable/system state, schemas, reconciliation state, integrations, automations, monitoring, and failures;
- downstream evidence that people or workflows retrieve, use, depend on, correct, or ignore the capability's outputs.

For System & Structure, these sources are primary observable evidence of the capability's actual current work.

## Evidence boundaries

Keep these distinctions explicit:

- artifact creation ≠ organisational adoption;
- GitHub commit ≠ organisational decision unless current authority supports that interpretation;
- Slack discussion ≠ validated organisational fact automatically;
- technical implementation ≠ organisational structure;
- tool presence ≠ demonstrated capability;
- activity ≠ successful outcome;
- intended mandate ≠ current capability reality.

## Capability Health

**Capability Health** is a bounded judgement about whether a capability is current, usable, aligned, monitored, transferable, and able to continue under its real dependencies.

Capability health is not inferred from activity volume.

Relevant questions include:

- Is the capability's current reality reconstructable?
- Are its maintained assets and system behaviour aligned with current authority?
- Are failures, drift, or stale assumptions visible?
- Does downstream work actually use the outputs?
- Does continuity still depend on one person?
- Can another person understand and continue the capability?

## Monitoring rule

Where a capability needs to remain reconstructable over time, repeated reconstruction should evolve into proportionate monitoring.

Monitor **material change**, not every action.

Surface a finding when evidence suggests:

- a recurring capability has appeared, changed, or stopped;
- authority/documentation drift;
- system/document mismatch;
- unresolved dependency;
- failure or unobserved automation;
- key-person continuity risk;
- repeated reconstruction that should become organisation-held;
- output produced but not used;
- use/outcome evidence contradicting the intended benefit.

No material change means no unnecessary report.

## Relationship to current methods

### Reconstruction & Reconciliation Method

Governs how distributed organisational and operational evidence, including capability evidence, becomes reliable reusable shared organisational information.

### Structure Method — Reality to Requirements

Uses relevant current DCA reality — Operational Reality, Capability Reality, or both — to derive justified organisational, information, structural, and system responses.

## Short rule

**Observe the capability through evidence of what it actually does and maintains; do not substitute intended mandate, artifact presence, implementation activity, or tool ownership for capability reality.**
